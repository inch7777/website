
There exists an upper bound of the amount of tears a human could ever cry out in their lifetime, and I already ran out of my quota before 7.  
  
Mother should be one’s first sight of woman. But my mother for me becomes every sight of woman. I see her in every woman and feel eager to fall into her arms and lie on her tender bosom and yet the same kind of aversion towards her rises and destructs and forces me to turn my head around. I was fed by her breast milk until 3. Is that a way to nourish or to control? Is that the reason why I cried day and night until utter exhaustion hit after she left me behind at 4? Is that a suggestion of love or the adoration for a pet? Am I her extension to reach her undone or an independent entity out of her? I am pretty sure that none of these questions have ever popped up in her mind, since she was and still is such an ignorant and shallow and blind person. But who cares as a 4-year-old kid? A child solely desires joy and love, and those lacking of joy and love as a child remain children. I am a child. Now I don’t know whether it’s a good thing or a bad thing. But it’s okay. It is what it is.  
  
My mother has become an indifferent stranger now, for time and the accompanying wounds along the passage of time is irreversible, and I had set out to find love and joy in other ways.

As I walked along the empty path across the woods near our campus, a girl seemingly of the same age appeared in front of me. She holds such grand eyes in sharp contrast with my mother’s slander narrow ones. She is called Wilky. I didn’t mean to bring my mother up, or compare them intentionally. It’s just that she nonsensically occupied some parts of me, and seemingly stuck to me as an internalized standard—she became my every sight of woman. Sometimes the child-like attributes my mother possesses would register straight in her eyes: hesitation, worry, skepticism, innocence, anger, disappointment. Those moments remind me of the self-evident fact that she is also but a child. She never even attempted to pretend. I love those pure, honest, authentic, naked registration of sentiment so much just as I love children, yet I barely can stand that ignorance as the other side of a coin. Her presumed blinding conviction of knowing me irritates me all the time, to a degree that I let go of my rationality suppressing the repulsion, unleash my resistant yelling against her, then hatred and disgust would take shape and twist my face into one of another being. It only happens between her and I. It might not be a proper private present for a mother but it suffices as a lifelong curse for a son.

However, it must be noted that not all children act naturally to get what they desire. Some of them gain attention by performing, and my mother sometimes is one of them. She would always pull out her phone to film the dishes on the dining table and me, intentionally delivering dull caring remarks “See, we have 4 dishes today; babe do you like them; eat as much as you want, so you will grow up faster”, a superficial, bragging, discernably pretentious tone in her voice, with awkwardly forced delight in her smile. The sense of being objectified enrages me: It seems to me like I am neither more than a mirror to reflect her glorious selfless motherly love nor a container of her cheap love instead of a real person.

I have never hugged her for more than 10 years, or put another way, I had only hugged her for 4 years. Hug is such a remote concept, just as remote as the streams flowing in Sahara Desert to the South of China. Hug gives a sense of home, and I have long been homeless. One cannot hug themselves, therefore pathetically no home can be found within them. I missed my home 13 years ago as I missed my attachment to my mother then. 

I always have that indifferent and nonchalant facial expression when I walk, so that others will not be able to speculate my mind, or maybe I just tried to do the opposite of my mom. So people usually don’t say hi to me. But Wilky did. Presumably she ignored my head in her sight. She saw a person without a face. And it’s exactly this that makes me like her. My mother, instead, would always endeavor to find something that she could comment or ask about on my face: Why do you look so tired baby? Why don’t you laugh honey? Your dark circles are getting heavier, have you had enough sleep? I always answered with “Yes” or “No” absentmindedly and tried to hold my anger back. Yet it disturbed me so much when I found myself asking the same type of questions to Wilky from some day on. Are they common concerns between loved ones? If so does it mean I didn’t love my mother so it irritated me, if not does it mean I am so affected by the shadow of my mother?
