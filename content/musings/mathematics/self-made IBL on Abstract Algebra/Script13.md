
# MATH 161, SHEET 13: GALOIS THEORY I — AUTOMORPHISMS AND THE GALOIS CORRESPONDENCE

This sheet introduces the fundamental objects of Galois theory: field automorphisms, fixed fields, and Galois groups. The centerpiece is the Galois correspondence, which establishes a duality between intermediate fields and subgroups of the Galois group.

**Commentary.** Galois theory is the crown jewel of abstract algebra. It establishes a dictionary between field extensions and group theory: intermediate fields correspond to subgroups of the Galois group. This correspondence allows us to translate problems about fields (like solving polynomial equations) into problems about groups (like determining solvability). The beauty of this correspondence is that it is a perfect duality—large fields correspond to small subgroups, and vice versa.

## Field Automorphisms

**Definition 13.1.** Let $E$ be a field. An **automorphism** of $E$ is an isomorphism $\sigma: E \to E$.

**Definition 13.2.** Let $E/F$ be a field extension. The **Galois group** of $E$ over $F$ is:
$$\operatorname{Gal}(E/F) = \{\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(E) \mid \sigma(a) = a \text{ for all } a \in F\}.$$

**Theorem 13.3.** $\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)$ is a group under composition.

**Example 13.4.** $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, generated by complex conjugation $\sigma(z) = \bar{z}$.

**Example 13.5.** $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, generated by $\sigma(\sqrt{2}) = -\sqrt{2}$.

**Example 13.6.** $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})/\mathbb{Q})$ is trivial, since $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$ contains only one root of $x^3 - 2$.

**Exercise 13.7.** Compute $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q})$.

**Exercise 13.8.** Compute $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})/\mathbb{Q})$.

**Exercise 13.9.** Show that $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2})/\mathbb{Q})$ is trivial.

## Fixed Fields

**Definition 13.10.** Let $E$ be a field and $H \subseteq \operatorname{Aut}(E)$ a subgroup. The **fixed field** of $H$ is:
$$E^H = \{a \in E \mid \sigma(a) = a \text{ for all } \sigma \in H\}.$$

**Theorem 13.11.** For any subgroup $H \subseteq \operatorname{Aut}(E)$, the fixed field $E^H$ is a subfield of $E$.

**Definition 13.12.** Let $E/F$ be a field extension and $K$ an intermediate field ($F \subseteq K \subseteq E$). The **fixing group** of $K$ is:
$$\operatorname{Gal}(E/K) = \{\sigma \in \operatorname{Gal}(E/F) \mid \sigma(a) = a \text{ for all } a \in K\}.$$

**Example 13.13.** In $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})$ with $G = \operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$:

- $E^{\{e\}} = E$
- $E^G = \mathbb{Q}$
- $E^{\langle \sigma \rangle} = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$, where $\sigma(\sqrt{2}) = -\sqrt{2}$
- $E^{\langle \tau \rangle} = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, where $\tau(\sqrt{3}) = -\sqrt{3}$

**Exercise 13.14.** Let $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega)$ be the splitting field of $x^3 - 2$. Find the fixed field of complex conjugation.

**Exercise 13.15.** Let $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$. What is $E^{\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})}$?

## The Conjugation Isomorphism

**Theorem 13.17 (Conjugation Isomorphism).** Let $F$ be a field, $K$ an extension field of $F$, and $\alpha, \beta \in K$ algebraic over $F$ with $\deg(\alpha, F) = n$. The map:
$$\psi_{\alpha,\beta}: F(\alpha) \to F(\beta), \qquad \psi_{\alpha,\beta}(c_0 + c_1\alpha + \cdots + c_{n-1}\alpha^{n-1}) = c_0 + c_1\beta + \cdots + c_{n-1}\beta^{n-1}$$
for $c_i \in F$, is an isomorphism of $F(\alpha)$ onto $F(\beta)$ iff $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are conjugate over $F$ (i.e., have the same minimal polynomial over $F$).

**Corollary 13.18.** If $\alpha, \beta$ are conjugate over $F$, then $F(\alpha) \cong F(\beta)$.

**Corollary 13.19.** Any isomorphism between simple extensions $F(\alpha)$ and $F(\beta)$ that fixes $F$ is completely determined by the image of $\alpha$, which must be a conjugate of $\alpha$.

## Normal and Separable Extensions

**Definition 13.22.** A finite extension $E/F$ is **normal** if every irreducible polynomial in $F[x]$ that has one root in $E$ splits completely in $E$.

**Definition 13.23.** A finite extension $E/F$ is **separable** if every element of $E$ has a minimal polynomial with distinct roots (equivalently, if the minimal polynomial has no multiple roots).

**Definition 13.24.** A finite extension $E/F$ is **Galois** if it is normal and separable. Equivalently, $E$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial over $F$.

**Theorem 13.25.** If $E/F$ is Galois, then:
$$|\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)| = [E:F].$$

**Theorem 13.26.** If $E/F$ is finite, then $|\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)| \leq [E:F]$, with equality iff $E/F$ is Galois.

**Example 13.27.** $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois since it is the splitting field of $x^2 - 2$.

**Example 13.28.** $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ is not Galois since it is not normal ($x^3 - 2$ has only one root in the field).

**Example 13.29.** $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega)/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois (splitting field of $x^3 - 2$).

**Exercise 13.30.** Show that $\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}$ is Galois. What is its Galois group?

**Exercise 13.31.** Determine whether $\mathbb{Q}(i)/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois. Justify your answer.

**Exercise 13.32.** Is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{2})/\mathbb{Q}$ Galois? Why or why not?

## The Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory

**Theorem 13.33 (Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory).** Let $E/F$ be a finite Galois extension with Galois group $G = \operatorname{Gal}(E/F)$. Then there is a bijection:
$$\{\text{intermediate fields } F \subseteq K \subseteq E\} \longleftrightarrow \{\text{subgroups } H \leq G\}$$
given by:
$$K \mapsto \operatorname{Gal}(E/K), \qquad H \mapsto E^H.$$

Moreover, this bijection has the following properties:

1. **Inclusion reversal:** $K_1 \subseteq K_2$ iff $\operatorname{Gal}(E/K_2) \leq \operatorname{Gal}(E/K_1)$.

2. **Degrees:** $[E:K] = |\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)|$ and $[K:F] = [G : \operatorname{Gal}(E/K)]$.

3. **Normality:** $K/F$ is Galois iff $\operatorname{Gal}(E/K) \triangleleft G$. In this case:
   $$\operatorname{Gal}(K/F) \cong G / \operatorname{Gal}(E/K).$$

**Example 13.34.** For $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{3})$ over $\mathbb{Q}$:

- $G \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ has 5 subgroups
- Intermediate fields: $\mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{6}), E$
- The correspondence is inclusion-reversing

**Example 13.35.** For $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \omega)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, $G \cong S_3$. The subgroups of $S_3$ correspond to intermediate fields:
- $A_3 \leftrightarrow \mathbb{Q}(\omega)$
- Each transposition $\leftrightarrow$ a field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2} \cdot \omega^k)$
- $S_3 \leftrightarrow \mathbb{Q}$

## The Galois Group of a Polynomial

**Definition 13.39.** Let $f(x) \in F[x]$ be a separable polynomial, and let $E$ be its splitting field over $F$. The **Galois group of $f$** is:
$$G_f = \operatorname{Gal}(E/F).$$

**Theorem 13.40.** If $f(x) \in F[x]$ is separable of degree $n$, then $G_f$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of the symmetric group $S_n$.

**Theorem 13.41.** If $f(x)$ is irreducible of degree $n$, then $G_f$ acts transitively on the roots of $f$. In particular, $|G_f|$ is divisible by $n$.

**Example 13.42.** For $f(x) = x^3 - 2 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, $G_f \cong S_3$.

**Example 13.43.** For $f(x) = x^4 - 4x^2 + 2 \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, $G_f \cong \mathbb{Z}_4$ or $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$.

**Exercise 13.44.** Determine the Galois group of $x^2 - 5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Exercise 13.45.** Determine the Galois group of $x^4 - 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Exercise 13.46.** Show that the Galois group of an irreducible cubic is either $A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}_3$ or $S_3$.

## The Alternating Group $A_5$ and the Non-Solvability of $S_5$

**Definition 13.47.** A group is **simple** if it has no nontrivial proper normal subgroups.

**Theorem 13.48.** The alternating group $A_5$ is simple.

**Proof.** $A_5$ is generated by 3-cycles. Any nontrivial normal subgroup of $A_5$ contains a 3-cycle. Since all 3-cycles are conjugate in $A_5$, such a subgroup contains all 3-cycles, hence is all of $A_5$.

**Theorem 13.49.** The symmetric group $S_n$ is not solvable for $n \geq 5$.

**Proof.** $A_5 \leq S_n$ for $n \geq 5$. If $S_n$ were solvable, then by Theorem 14.3 (subgroups of solvable groups are solvable), $A_5$ would be solvable. But $A_5$ is nonabelian and simple, so its only normal series is $\{e\} \triangleleft A_5$, with quotient $A_5$ nonabelian. Hence $A_5$ is not solvable. Therefore $S_n$ is not solvable.
