# MATH 161, SHEET 8: IDEALS AND QUOTIENT RINGS

This sheet introduces ideals—the ring-theoretic analogue of normal subgroups—and quotient rings. Ideals are exactly the subrings that arise as kernels of ring homomorphisms, and they are precisely the substructures we can "quotient by" to form new rings.

**Commentary.** In group theory, normal subgroups are exactly the subgroups that arise as kernels of homomorphisms, and they are precisely the subgroups we can quotient by to form factor groups. In ring theory, ideals play the exact same role. The First, Second, and Third Isomorphism Theorems for rings mirror those for groups. This parallel structure is one of the great unifying themes of abstract algebra.

## Ideals

**Definition 8.1.** Let $R$ be a ring. A nonempty subset $I \subseteq R$ is a **(two-sided) ideal** if:

1. $I$ is a subgroup of $(R, +)$. Equivalently, for all $a, b \in I$: $a - b \in I$.

2. $I$ absorbs multiplication by elements of $R$: for all $a \in I$ and $r \in R$:
   $$r a \in I \quad \text{and} \quad a r \in I.$$

If only $r a \in I$ holds, $I$ is a **left ideal**. If only $a r \in I$ holds, $I$ is a **right ideal**.

**Definition 8.2.** An ideal $I \subset R$ is **proper** if $I \neq R$. The **trivial ideals** are $\{0\}$ and $R$.

**Example 8.3.** In $\mathbb{Z}$, every ideal is of the form $n\mathbb{Z}$ for some $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. These are the principal ideals.

**Example 8.4.** In any ring $R$ and any element $a \in R$, the set
$$(a) = \{ra \mid r \in R\}$$
is a left ideal. If $R$ is commutative, it is a two-sided ideal, called the **principal ideal generated by $a$**.

**Example 8.5.** The set of even integers $2\mathbb{Z}$ is an ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$.

**Example 8.6.** In $M_2(\mathbb{R})$, the set of matrices with first column zero is a left ideal but not a right ideal.

**Example 8.7.** The set of polynomials with zero constant term is an ideal of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$.

**Example 8.8.** In $\mathbb{Z}_6$, the set $\{0, 2, 4\}$ is an ideal. It is the principal ideal generated by $2$.

**Theorem 8.9.** The kernel of any ring homomorphism $\varphi: R \to S$ is an ideal of $R$.

**Theorem 8.10.** If $I \subseteq R$ is an ideal, then the quotient group $R/I$ (with coset addition) can be made into a ring by defining:
$$(a + I)(b + I) = ab + I.$$

**Exercise 8.11.** Verify that $n\mathbb{Z}$ is an ideal of $\mathbb{Z}$ for every $n$.

**Exercise 8.12.** Show that the intersection of any collection of ideals of $R$ is again an ideal.

**Exercise 8.13.** Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity. Show that the ideal generated by $a \in R$ is
$$(a) = \{ra \mid r \in R\}.$$

**Exercise 8.14.** Give an example of a left ideal in $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ that is not a right ideal.

**Exercise 8.15.** Prove Theorem 8.9.

## Operations on Ideals

**Definition 8.16.** Let $I, J$ be ideals of a ring $R$.

- The **sum** of $I$ and $J$ is: $I + J = \{a + b \mid a \in I, b \in J\}$.
- The **product** of $I$ and $J$ is: $IJ = \{\sum_{k=1}^n a_k b_k \mid a_k \in I, b_k \in J, n \in \mathbb{Z}^+\}$.
- The **intersection** of $I$ and $J$ is: $I \cap J = \{a \mid a \in I \text{ and } a \in J\}$.

**Theorem 8.17.** $I + J$, $IJ$, and $I \cap J$ are ideals of $R$.

**Theorem 8.18.** $I + J$ is the smallest ideal containing both $I$ and $J$.

**Theorem 8.19.** $I \cap J$ is the largest ideal contained in both $I$ and $J$.

**Theorem 8.20.** $IJ \subseteq I \cap J$. If $I + J = R$, then $IJ = I \cap J$.

**Exercise 8.23.** Let $I = (2)$ and $J = (3)$ in $\mathbb{Z}$. Compute $I + J$, $IJ$, and $I \cap J$. Verify that $IJ = I \cap J$.

## Quotient Rings

**Definition 8.24.** Let $I$ be an ideal of $R$. The **quotient ring** (or **factor ring**) $R/I$ is the set of cosets:
$$R/I = \{a + I \mid a \in R\}$$
with operations:
$$(a + I) + (b + I) = (a + b) + I,$$
$$(a + I)(b + I) = ab + I.$$

**Theorem 8.25.** If $I$ is an ideal of $R$, then $R/I$ is a ring.

**Theorem 8.26 (Natural Homomorphism).** The map
$$\pi: R \to R/I, \qquad \pi(a) = a + I$$
is a surjective ring homomorphism with kernel $I$.

~={orange}**Example 8.27.** $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}_n$.=~

~={orange}**Example 8.28.** If $R = \mathbb{Z}[x]$ and $I = (x)$, then $R/I \cong \mathbb{Z}$.=~

~={orange}**Example 8.29.** If $R = \mathbb{Z}[x]$ and $I = (x^2 + 1)$, then $R/I \cong \mathbb{Z}[i]$.=~

~={orange}**Example 8.30.** If $R = \mathbb{R}[x]$ and $I = (x^2 + 1)$, then $R/I \cong \mathbb{C}$.=~

~={orange}**Exercise 8.31.** Let $I = \{0, 2, 4\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_6$. Compute $R/I$ and identify it with a familiar ring.=~

~={orange}**Exercise 8.32.** Show that $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2 + 1) \cong \mathbb{C}$.=~

## Prime and Maximal Ideals

**Definition 8.34.** Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity. A proper ideal $P \subset R$ is a **prime ideal** if:
$$ab \in P \implies a \in P \text{ or } b \in P.$$

**Definition 8.35.** A proper ideal $M \subset R$ is a **maximal ideal** if there is no proper ideal $I$ with $M \subset I \subset R$. Equivalently, $M$ is maximal among proper ideals.

~={orange}**Theorem 8.36.** Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity. An ideal $P$ is prime iff $R/P$ is an integral domain.=~

~={orange}**Theorem 8.37.** Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity. An ideal $M$ is maximal iff $R/M$ is a field.=~

**Corollary 8.38.** Every maximal ideal is prime.

**Corollary 8.39.** In a finite commutative ring with unity, every prime ideal is maximal.

**Example 8.40.** In $\mathbb{Z}$, the prime ideals are $\{0\}$ and $p\mathbb{Z}$ for primes $p$. The maximal ideals are exactly $p\mathbb{Z}$ for primes $p$.

**Example 8.41.** In $\mathbb{Z}[x]$, the ideal $(x)$ is prime but not maximal.

**Example 8.42.** In $\mathbb{R}[x]$, the ideal $(x^2 + 1)$ is maximal (since $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2 + 1) \cong \mathbb{C}$).

**Example 8.43.** In $\mathbb{Z}_6$, the prime ideals are $(2)$ and $(3)$, both maximal.

**Exercise 8.44.** Let $R = \mathbb{Z}_6$. Find all prime ideals and all maximal ideals of $R$.

**Exercise 8.45.** Show that in $\mathbb{Z}$, every nonzero prime ideal is maximal.

**Exercise 8.46.** Prove that in a finite commutative ring with unity, every prime ideal is maximal.

## The Correspondence Theorem for Rings

**Theorem 8.49 (Correspondence Theorem).** Let $I$ be an ideal of $R$, and let $\pi: R \to R/I$ be the natural homomorphism. There is a bijection between:
- Ideals $J$ of $R$ with $I \subseteq J \subseteq R$, and
- Ideals $\overline{J}$ of $R/I$.

given by:
$$J \mapsto \pi(J) = J/I, \qquad \overline{J} \mapsto \pi^{-1}(\overline{J}).$$

~={orange}Moreover, this bijection preserves inclusions, sums, intersections, products, and the properties of being prime or maximal.=~

**Corollary 8.50.** The ideals of $R/I$ are exactly the ideals $J/I$ where $J$ is an ideal of $R$ containing $I$.

**Exercise 8.51.** Let $R = \mathbb{Z}_{12}$ and $I = \langle 3 \rangle$. Use the Correspondence Theorem to list all ideals of $R/I$.

**Exercise 8.52.** Let $I$ be an ideal of $R$. Prove that $R/I$ is a field iff $I$ is maximal.

~={orange}## The Isomorphism Theorems for Rings=~

**Theorem 8.53 (First Isomorphism Theorem for Rings).** If $\varphi: R \to S$ is a ring homomorphism, then
$$R / \ker \varphi \cong \operatorname{im} \varphi.$$

**Corollary 8.54.** If $\varphi: R \to S$ is a surjective ring homomorphism, then
$$R / \ker \varphi \cong S.$$

**Example 8.55.** The map $\varphi: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}_n$ given by $\varphi(m) = m \bmod n$ has kernel $n\mathbb{Z}$. Hence $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}_n$.

**Example 8.56.** The map $\varphi: \mathbb{Z}[x] \to \mathbb{Z}$ given by $\varphi(f(x)) = f(0)$ has kernel $(x)$. Hence $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

**Theorem 8.57 (Second Isomorphism Theorem for Rings).** Let $R$ be a ring, $A$ a subring, and $I$ an ideal of $R$. Then $A + I$ is a subring of $R$, $A \cap I$ is an ideal of $A$, and:
$$(A + I)/I \cong A/(A \cap I).$$

**Theorem 8.58 (Third Isomorphism Theorem for Rings).** Let $R$ be a ring, and let $I \subseteq J$ be ideals of $R$. Then $J/I$ is an ideal of $R/I$, and:
$$(R/I)/(J/I) \cong R/J.$$

**Exercise 8.59.** Use the First Isomorphism Theorem to prove that $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

**Exercise 8.60.** Let $\varphi: \mathbb{R}[x] \to \mathbb{C}$ be given by $\varphi(f(x)) = f(i)$. Identify $\ker \varphi$ and $\operatorname{im} \varphi$, and verify the First Isomorphism Theorem.

## Chinese Remainder Theorem for Rings

**Definition 8.63.** Two ideals $I, J$ of a commutative ring $R$ are **comaximal** if $I + J = R$.

**Theorem 8.64 (Chinese Remainder Theorem for Rings).** Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity, and let $I_1, \ldots, I_n$ be pairwise comaximal ideals. Then:
$$R/(I_1 \cap \cdots \cap I_n) \cong R/I_1 \times \cdots \times R/I_n.$$

**Corollary 8.65.** If $I_1, \ldots, I_n$ are pairwise comaximal, then:
$$I_1 \cap \cdots \cap I_n = I_1 I_2 \cdots I_n.$$

**Example 8.66.** In $\mathbb{Z}$, $(2)$ and $(3)$ are comaximal since $(2)+(3)=\mathbb{Z}$. The CRT gives:
$$\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}.$$

**Exercise 8.67.** Prove CRT for two ideals $I, J$ with $I+J=R$ by defining $\varphi: R \to R/I \times R/J$ and using the First Isomorphism Theorem.

**Exercise 8.68.** Use CRT to show that $\mathbb{Z}_{mn} \cong \mathbb{Z}_m \times \mathbb{Z}_n$ when $\gcd(m,n)=1$.

## Applications

**Theorem 8.69.** A commutative ring with unity is a field iff its only ideals are $\{0\}$ and itself.

**Theorem 8.70.** Every maximal ideal is the kernel of a homomorphism onto a field.

**Theorem 8.71.** In a PID (principal ideal domain), an ideal $(a)$ is maximal iff $a$ is irreducible.

**Theorem 8.72.** In a PID, an ideal $(a)$ is prime iff $a = 0$ or $a$ is irreducible.

**Exercise 8.73.** Prove that $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ is not a PID by showing that the ideal $(2, x)$ is not principal.

**Exercise 8.74.** Show that $\mathbb{Q}[x]/(x^2 - 2) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$.

**Exercise 8.75.** Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity. Prove that if $R$ has no nontrivial ideals, then $R$ is a field.
