# MATH 161, SHEET 3.5: HOMOMORPHISMS AND ISOMORPHISMS

This sheet introduces homomorphisms—the structure-preserving maps between groups—and isomorphisms, the maps that identify when two groups are structurally identical. Homomorphisms are the foundation for the isomorphism theorems (Sheet 4), Cayley's theorem (Sheet 5), and Galois theory (Sheets 13-15).

**Commentary.** In mathematics, we study objects and the maps between them that preserve their structure. For groups, these maps are homomorphisms. They allow us to compare groups, relate them to each other, and understand their internal structure through kernels and images. The First Isomorphism Theorem (Sheet 4) is the culmination of this idea: every homomorphic image of a group is a quotient of that group.

## Homomorphisms

**Definition 3.5.1.** Let $(G, \cdot)$ and $(H, *)$ be groups. A function $\varphi: G \to H$ is a **homomorphism** if for all $a, b \in G$:
$$\varphi(a \cdot b) = \varphi(a) * \varphi(b).$$

**Example 3.5.2.** The map $\varphi: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}_n$ given by $\varphi(m) = m \bmod n$ is a homomorphism.

**Example 3.5.3.** The sign map $\operatorname{sgn}: S_n \to \{\pm 1\}$ defined by $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = 1$ if $\sigma$ is even and $-1$ if $\sigma$ is odd is a homomorphism.

**Definition 3.5.4.** The **kernel** of a homomorphism $\varphi: G \to H$ is:
$$\ker(\varphi) = \{g \in G \mid \varphi(g) = e_H\}.$$

**Definition 3.5.5.** The **image** of a homomorphism $\varphi: G \to H$ is:
$$\operatorname{im}(\varphi) = \{\varphi(g) \mid g \in G\}.$$

**Theorem 3.5.6.** If $\varphi: G \to H$ is a homomorphism, then:

1. $\varphi(e_G) = e_H$.
2. $\varphi(g^{-1}) = \varphi(g)^{-1}$ for all $g \in G$.
3. $\varphi(g^n) = \varphi(g)^n$ for all $g \in G$ and $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Theorem 3.5.7.** If $\varphi: G \to H$ is a homomorphism, then:

1. $\ker(\varphi)$ is a normal subgroup of $G$.
2. $\operatorname{im}(\varphi)$ is a subgroup of $H$.

**Theorem 3.5.8.** A homomorphism $\varphi: G \to H$ is injective iff $\ker(\varphi) = \{e_G\}$.

## Isomorphisms

**Definition 3.5.13.** A homomorphism $\varphi: G \to H$ is an **isomorphism** if it is bijective. If such a map exists, we write $G \cong H$ and say $G$ is **isomorphic** to $H$.

**Theorem 3.5.14.** If $\varphi: G \to H$ is an isomorphism, then $\varphi^{-1}: H \to G$ is also an isomorphism.

**Theorem 3.5.15.** If $\varphi: G \to H$ and $\psi: H \to K$ are isomorphisms, then $\psi \circ \varphi: G \to K$ is an isomorphism.

**Theorem 3.5.16.** An isomorphism preserves:

1. The identity element.
2. Inverses.
3. The order of every element.
4. The property of being abelian.
5. The property of being cyclic.
6. The property of being generated by a set.

**Theorem 3.5.17 (Invariance of Group Properties).** If $G \cong H$, then $G$ and $H$ have the same cardinality, same element orders, same number of subgroups of each order, and the same group-theoretic structure.

**Example 3.5.18.** $\mathbb{Z} \cong 2\mathbb{Z}$ via $\varphi(n) = 2n$.

**Example 3.5.19.** $\mathbb{Z}_4$ and $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ are **not** isomorphic: one has an element of order 4, the other does not.

**Exercise 3.5.21.** Show that $\mathbb{Q}$ under addition is **not** isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$ under addition.

## Automorphisms

**Definition 3.5.23.** An **automorphism** of a group $G$ is an isomorphism $\varphi: G \to G$. The set of all automorphisms of $G$ is denoted $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$.

**Theorem 3.5.24.** $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is a group under composition.

**Definition 3.5.25.** For $g \in G$, the **inner automorphism** induced by $g$ is:
$$\iota_g: G \to G, \qquad \iota_g(x) = g x g^{-1}.$$

**Theorem 3.5.26.** Every inner automorphism is an automorphism. The set of all inner automorphisms, denoted $\operatorname{Inn}(G)$, is a subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$.

**Theorem 3.5.27.** $\operatorname{Inn}(G) \triangleleft \operatorname{Aut}(G)$.

**Example 3.5.28.** For an abelian group $G$, every inner automorphism is the identity map. So $\operatorname{Inn}(G) = \{e\}$.

**Example 3.5.29.** The map $\varphi: \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}$ defined by $\varphi(n) = -n$ is an automorphism.

**Exercise 3.5.31.** Show that $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$.

**Exercise 3.5.32.** Show that $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}_n) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^\times$.
